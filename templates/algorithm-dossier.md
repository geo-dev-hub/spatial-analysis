## Start with the question

Open with a familiar situation, question or decision that makes the method matter. Use ordinary language first. Avoid opening with a formal definition or a list of prerequisites.

## What are we trying to do?

Explain the problem, the inputs, the output and the scope. Distinguish nearby tasks that are easy to confuse. State what this record does not cover.

## The simple idea

Explain the central idea in a few sentences. Use a small example that a reader can picture.

**Choose the visual that does the job best.** Reuse an accurate source figure when its exact licence permits the intended use. Otherwise link to it or make an original visual that explains something specific. Use SVG for precise geometry, Mermaid for process and decision logic, a map for geographic context, and a plot for numerical behaviour. Add a useful caption, alt text and attribution. Follow [the visual reuse standard](../docs/visual-sources-and-attribution.md).

## The short version

Give the reader a compact explanation to remember before going deeper. Keep it technically accurate.

## Formal foundations

Define the objects, notation, assumptions and mathematical formulation.

Use GitHub-compatible Markdown math:
- inline: `$A \cap B$`;
- display: `$$ ... $$`.

Define every symbol close to its first use. State the assumptions and explain what the equation means in ordinary language. Do not use screenshots for equations when rendered notation is available. Check the rendered page on GitHub. Follow [the mathematics and visuals guide](../docs/math-and-visuals.md).

## How it works

Describe the method step by step. Add pseudocode where it clarifies the logic. Use a flowchart, sequence diagram, annotated geometry or state diagram when it makes a difficult step easier to follow.

## Correctness and guarantees

What does the method guarantee? Under which assumptions? Is it exact, approximate, heuristic, deterministic or stochastic? Separate the mathematical property from the behaviour of a named software implementation.

## Complexity

State time and space complexity, define the input-size variables and identify the computational model. Distinguish worst-case, expected and amortised bounds. Keep theoretical analysis separate from measured performance.

## Numerical, spatial and data considerations

Cover the issues that matter for this method: precision, tolerances, CRS, units, topology, geometry validity, missing data, scale, uncertainty, privacy, or other relevant assumptions. Do not add irrelevant checklist items just to fill a section.

## Work through an example

Use a small reproducible example. Show intermediate steps when they help the reader understand why the answer is what it is. State expected output and explain it.

## Try it yourself

Give exercises at more than one level when useful:
- **Notice:** identify the idea in a simple case.
- **Predict:** work out the result before running anything.
- **Trace:** follow the method step by step.
- **Change:** vary an input, parameter or assumption.
- **Build:** implement a small part or compare approaches.
- **Investigate:** design a test or find a limitation.

State what the learner should do and how they can check their answer. Keep solutions separate when that supports learning.

Link to tested code, notebooks, maps, datasets, plots or interactive demonstrations when they add genuine learning value. A notebook should let readers change something meaningful, inspect the result and understand what changed. Do not add interactivity as decoration.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

Compare named alternatives using criteria supported by sources or reproducible experiments. Explain when one is a better fit and what is sacrificed.

## Where it is useful

Connect the method to practical questions and real applications. Be specific about what it can and cannot tell someone.

## References

List primary papers, formal specifications, maintainer documentation and reliable secondary sources. For each important source, make clear which claim it supports. Record version and access date for documentation that changes over time.

## What remains uncertain

List unverified claims, unresolved assumptions, implementation gaps and useful next experiments. Do not hide uncertainty.
