# Learning paths

Learning paths are curated sequences through canonical records. They do not own or duplicate the technical content.

## Principles

- A learner may begin at the foundations, enter through a domain, or follow a practical project.
- Paths may overlap and may order the same algorithms differently.
- Publication edition numbers do not dictate learning order.
- Each step links to a canonical record and explains why it appears here.
- Prerequisites should be explicit, but avoid unnecessary gatekeeping.

## Initial path candidates

These are proposed routes, not yet validated curricula:

1. **Core GIS foundations** — geometry, spatial relationships, indexing, proximity, networks and routing.
2. **Computational geometry** — predicates, intersections, polygon operations, triangulation, Voronoi and geometric structures.
3. **Spatial databases** — spatial data models, indexes, query processing, joins and distributed execution.
4. **Spatial statistics** — sampling, autocorrelation, interpolation, geostatistics, uncertainty and spatiotemporal methods.
5. **Remote sensing and raster analysis** — raster foundations, image processing, classification, change detection and fusion.
6. **Urban and transport analysis** — networks, accessibility, flows, location, land use and simulation.

A path becomes active only when its sequence, prerequisites, outcomes and exercises have been reviewed.
